About the Project

A 1914 flat on the slope of Russian Hill, built as the city rose again after the fire, and warmed a century later for a slower, easier way of living.

It went up in the years Russian Hill was rebuilding, part of a neighborhood made to last. Today it sits a block off Polk Street, close enough to hear the city humming and far enough to close the door on it. The bones were good from the start. What the house needed was more room and less noise, so we turned the unused attic into a bedroom tucked under the roofline and let the rest of the house breathe.

Inside, pale walls catch the light from the open bay windows and carry it deep into each room. A few strong pieces do the work rather than many: a tufted leather ottoman made for feet up, a deep cream sofa you sink into and don’t leave, walnut and steel shelving holding the small things worth keeping. And green, everywhere, softening the corners and keeping every room alive.

Good bones deserve a light hand.

Materials

Made to be touched.

Tufted leather beside cream velvet. Reclaimed wood resting on blackened steel. The cool, pebbled grain of shagreen nesting tables. Capiz and glass-ball lighting that glows rather than glares, and a living palette of bird of paradise, air plants and potted palms.

In the end it’s a home at ease with the city around it: room enough for a table full of friends, quiet enough for a slow Sunday with the windows thrown open. Nothing shouts. Everything gets better with use.
