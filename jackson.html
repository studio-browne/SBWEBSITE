About the Project

Built in 1895 for Henry Lund, a German-born royal consul to Norway and Sweden, to a design by William H. Lillie.

Mahogany doors, a marble stair, a mosaic landing, quarter-sawn oak underfoot — all of it intact, and all of it gone quiet. Not ruined. Unloved. We took down two walls on the ground floor, which is not something we do lightly, and let the house breathe as one room again.

Stale, and stuck in its own past. It needed a refresh — or maybe an exorcism.

The client did not want to paint. We got them to anyway.

The Stair

Untouched.

La Farge stained glass above the original window seat, a Juliet balcony at the top of the run, and a copper-leaf ceiling over the landing — all original to the house, and none of it ours to improve.

Lillie got it right the first time.

The Ground Floor

Two walls, gone.

The hardest call on the job — removing original partitions so the ground floor could finally read as one room. The dining room was sized for the kind of entertaining a consul did in 1895.

A modern plan with 1895 manners.

Patina, provenance, and a few good quirks.

The Garden

Out the back.

Tall ferns, Japanese maples, a stone terrace and a level lawn, under London plane trees out front.
